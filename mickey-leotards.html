Mickey is a custom leotard designer based in Petaling Jaya, Malaysia. She got into it by chance — a friend with a daughter in rhythmic gymnastics asked her to do an alteration, and Mickey realised there was a gap in the market: plenty of local designers make custom evening and bridal wear, but almost no one makes custom leotards. That's when she decided to build a business around it.

The Challenge

About two years ago, Mickey was running a booth at a bazaar in Starhill when a foreign tourist tried to buy a leotard. The customer's card wouldn't go through — the payment gateway Mickey was using at the time only supported local payment methods, not international credit cards.

Frustrated, the customer walked away, and Mickey lost the sale on the spot. It was the moment that made clear how much a limited payment setup could cost her, especially at in-person events with tourists and one-off buyers.

The Solution

That lost sale sent Mickey looking for a payment option that wouldn't turn away a customer's card again. She found HitPay's card terminal — small enough to bring to every bazaar and pop-up, and able to accept the international cards her old gateway couldn't.

"The fact that I can take it around — the terminal is so small and compact."
— Mickey, Custom Leotard Designer

Tap to Pay on iPhone meant Mickey didn't need a second terminal to run two payment points at once. "The best thing now is I can do it on my phone itself," she said — while a colleague runs the terminal, she takes payments straight from her own phone at the same booth. She's since linked HitPay to her website too, opening her leotards up to international orders beyond the events she attends in person.

"Since I got HitPay, I've got a website as well. I link my HitPay to my website as the payment gateway, so we can get international orders through it."
— Mickey, Custom Leotard Designer

The Results

Two years on, Mickey no longer has to turn away a sale because of how someone wants to pay. Card, Tap to Pay, or online checkout — she now covers the ways her customers, local and international, actually want to buy.

For Mickey, it's also about something bigger than any one sale. She sees a retail landscape where malls increasingly look identical — the same H&M, the same Zara — and believes independent designers need active support to keep existing alongside the international brands, not be replaced by them.

"If you don't have a group of people actively who want to do that, you will lose that group of people, and then you will just have the international brands everywhere. I think it's important to have a creative community and to build that."
